Why the $3,800 listing is not a benchmark

It is tempting to look at a neighbor asking $3,800 and conclude there is room above $3,150. We would caution strongly against that reading.

  • It has not leased either. Ten days, same as yours, same result. A price nobody has paid is not evidence of value.
  • It is not supported by the analysis. When we set your price we looked at a broader set of Lake Nona homes, including larger ones, to establish what this product actually commands. $3,800 sits well outside what that analysis supports at this size.
  • That owner closed on the home in August. A brand-new investor with a fresh mortgage and an aspirational asking price is a very common pattern, and those listings typically correct after a month or two of silence — often sharply.

The risk of anchoring to it is real. If we hold at $3,150 because $3,800 makes us look reasonable, and that listing drops to $3,100 in October, we have spent six weeks of vacancy to end up behind where we started.

The most important number in this report is zero. No home in this segment has leased in the MLS. We have no proof of demand at $3,800, at $3,150, or at any price — only two listings sitting side by side, both quiet. In that situation the correct move is not to match the other listing. It is to find the price where the phone rings.

Section Four

How we arrived at $3,150, and what has changed

We want you to see that the original price was reasoned, not guessed — and why we are now recommending we move off it.

When we priced this home there were no leased comparables for this product, because it did not exist yet. So we widened the analysis, looking at a broader set of Lake Nona homes including larger ones, and set $3,150 as a defensible number against what that range supported. It was the right call with the information available at the time.

What we did not have then, and do have now, is eleven days of live market response. That is real data, and it outranks any comparable analysis. The market has now told us something that no spreadsheet could: at $3,150, renters are not finding this home.

The $3,000 line

Renters do not browse by square footage or by finish level. They set a maximum price and browse what comes back. On every major portal, the filters step in round numbers, and $3,000 is one of the most heavily used ceilings there is.

At $3,150 your home does not appear in a single search capped at $3,000. Not lower in the results — absent from them entirely. The photographs, the EV charger, the smart locks, the amenity package, none of it is ever seen, because the listing was filtered out before it could be.

That is the most plausible explanation for one inquiry in eleven days on a home this attractive.

Our recommendation

We recommend reducing to:

$2,995

A reduction of $155 per month, and a deliberate move below the $3,000 line.

Three reasons, in order of importance.

It puts your home in front of renters who cannot currently see it. Every search capped at $3,000 excludes your listing today. That is likely the single largest pool of renters shopping this price range, and at $2,995 your home enters all of it. This is the entire argument for why $155 buys far more than $155 of value.

It makes the comparison with your neighbor decisive rather than merely favorable. At $3,150 you are 17% below the competing listing. At $2,995 you are $805 per month — 21% — below it, on a larger home. A renter weighing the two does not need to deliberate. That is the position we want you in while you are the only real option in this community.

It acts while the listing is still new. Eleven days in, your listing still carries the visibility a fresh listing gets, and a price change triggers a price-reduced flag that pushes it back to the top of recently-updated results. Making this move now compounds two advantages. Making it in October means correcting a stale listing with neither of them.

What waiting costs. Every thirty days vacant costs $3,150 in rent you never recover — $105 for every day the home sits empty. A reduction costs a fraction of that:
